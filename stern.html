The degree — B.S. in Business, NYU Stern

Researched Nov 24, 2026, from NYU Stern's official pages (stern.nyu.edu) and the NYU University Bulletin (bulletins.nyu.edu) only. Course names and codes copied as published; where two official pages print a title or code differently, both versions are recorded and flagged at the end. Stern's own disclaimer: "Official BS in Business curriculum information is exclusively in the University Bulletin."

Degree: Bachelor of Science in Business (B.S. in Business), NYU Stern Undergraduate College

Shape: 128 total units; a concentration requires at least 12 credits of designated advanced coursework

Rule: declare at least one concentration (no more than two) by the end of junior year

Note: Stern also publishes standing rules on every concentration page — a Stern elective course may satisfy only one concentration requirement, and juniors/seniors may request enrollment in MBA (-GB) courses via the MBA Course Request Form

Published course descriptions (as published in official Stern syllabi / the NYU bulletin; one to two lines each)

FINC-UB 38 Real Estate Capital Markets — "Real Estate Capital Markets studies debt and equity secondary markets linked to real estate assets. … The course places considerable emphasis on securitization, the housing finance system, the GSEs, the subprime mortgage crisis and related topics." (Course syllabus, Fall 2025–2026; the syllabus also states: "The only formal pre-requisite for this course is Foundations of Finance.")

FINC-UB 39 Real Estate Primary Markets — "There are two primary goals of this class: 1. To expose you to the terms, issues, and topics in commercial real estate; and 2. To give you the basic skills and intuition you need to begin to evaluate a variety of real estate investments." The syllabus calls it "the survey course in the Real Estate Department" providing "a broad overview of the real estate field." (Course syllabus, Spring 2025; bulletin prerequisite: Foundations of Finance, FINC-UB 2 or equivalent.)

FINC-UB 35 Real Estate Investment Strategies — "The course addresses public and private providers of real estate debt and equity capital and how this capital is channeled into commercial real estate." Its stated goal is "to give students in-depth exposure to current trading strategies in real estate capital markets and teach the tools to analyze their own trading ideas." (Course syllabus, Spring 2020, cross-listed FINC-GB 2341/FINC-UB 35.)

FINC-GB 6036 Real Estate Data Science, Artificial Intelligence, & Machine Learning — no standalone prose description was found on a Stern or bulletin page for the FINC-GB 6036 listing (see Flags). The identically titled undergraduate version, FINC-UB 36, is described in NYU's IMA Interchange course listing as: "In this highly applied course, students will be introduced to major data analysis and machine learning platforms; a wide range of public and private real estate and urban data sources; approaches to exploratory data analysis, real estate data visualization, and communication of findings; applied statistical modeling, including forecast modeling; and, emerging and prospective real estate applications for artificial intelligence and machine learning."

OPMG-UB 60 Real Estate Development & Entrepreneurship — "This course will introduce students to the broad aspects of real estate development from an entrepreneurial perspective. It is directed to students interested in real estate development from the point of view of three classes of investors…" (Course syllabus; the bulletin course description begins: "This course will introduce students to the operating process of real estate development from an entrepreneurial perspective.") Topics as published include all types of development: residential, hotel, office, retail, land and industrial properties.

MULT-UB 51 Real Estate Transactions — "This course is designed as an introduction to real estate transactions and the law for business school students. Students will be guided, step by step, through each phase of the life cycle of a typical real estate transaction from acquisition to disposition." (Syllabus for the cross-listed "Real Estate Transactions and Law" course, Fall 2025; bulletin prerequisites for MULT-UB 51: SOIM-UB 65 and senior standing, with ECON-UB 11 or equivalent as corequisite.)

BSPA-GB 2300 Real Estate Law & Transactions — the same Fall 2025 "Real Estate Transactions and Law" syllabus (the course is cross-listed for undergraduates as MULT-UB 51 and for MBA students under BSPA-GB): "This course is designed as an introduction to real estate transactions and the law for business school students… Focusing on single-asset transactions, this course includes the basics of commercial real estate law from structuring, letters of intent and acquisition through disposition, whether by sale or as a result of foreclosure."

FINC-UB 37 Real Estate Global Markets: Mexico City — "This course introduces students to real estate finance and investment analysis in non-US settings, as well as special issues when developing equity and debt capital internationally, and approaches to analyzing global real estate portfolios through the case study of Mexico." (Stern Around the World in Mexico course page, Spring 2026; a Stern Around the World course held in New York with a required field intensive trip to Mexico City; prerequisite FINC-UB 2; satisfies the Global Experience Requirement.)

Concentration — Actuarial Science

Status: eliminated as an option for students who entered in fall 2024 and going forward (see the note under the concentration list); the requirements below remain published for students who entered Stern before fall 2024. The page notes students can study actuarial topics, including preparation for a number of the professional exams, via the Statistics concentration. As published, the curriculum prepares students for the first four examinations of the Society of Actuaries and the Casualty Actuarial Society and satisfies the three Validation by Educational Experience (VEE) areas: Economics, Finance, and Applied Statistical Methods.

13. Not every concentration is 12 credits: Actuarial Science requires 31 credits and Econometrics & Quantitative Economics at least 23 credits, as published.

14. Entrepreneurship is one of the 12 on the degree page but is styled a "Co-Concentration" on its own page.

15. No prose course description for FINC-GB 6036 was found on a Stern or bulletin page; the description recorded above is for the identically titled FINC-UB 36, from NYU's IMA Interchange listing (an NYU site, not a Stern or bulletin page).

The bulletin's FINC-UB, MULT-UB, and OPMG-UB course pages publish credits, offering pattern, grading, and prerequisites but no prose descriptions for most courses; the Real Estate descriptions above therefore come from official Stern course syllabi and the Stern Around the World course page, as noted per course.
